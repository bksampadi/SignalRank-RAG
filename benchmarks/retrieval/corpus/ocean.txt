Ocean currents move large volumes of seawater across long distances and redistribute thermal energy around the planet. Warm currents can carry heat toward cooler regions, while cold currents can transport cooler water toward the tropics. This movement influences regional climate, especially near coastlines.

Currents are driven by several mechanisms, including wind, differences in water density, Earth's rotation, and the shape of ocean basins. Because the ocean stores and transports so much heat, changes in circulation can alter weather patterns even far from where the current originates.
