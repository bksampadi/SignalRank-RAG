Volcanoes form where molten rock, gases, and fragments from inside Earth reach the surface. Magma can rise through fractures in the crust, accumulate in chambers, and erupt as lava, ash, or explosive mixtures of gas and rock. The character of an eruption depends partly on magma composition and gas content.

Scientists monitor active volcanoes using seismic activity, ground deformation, gas emissions, and thermal measurements. An increase in small earthquakes or swelling of the ground can indicate that magma is moving beneath the surface, although such signals do not guarantee that an eruption will occur.
