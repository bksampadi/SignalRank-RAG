Ancient Greek mythology described a large family of gods associated with natural forces, social institutions, crafts, warfare, love, wisdom, and the fate of human beings. Zeus was commonly portrayed as ruler of the Olympian gods, while Hera, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Aphrodite, Poseidon, and others had distinct domains and traditions.

The myths were not a single fixed canon. Stories differed across regions, poets, cults, and historical periods. Greek gods appeared in epic poetry, theater, local religious practice, art, and political symbolism, so the same deity could have different attributes or stories in different contexts.
