Pharaohs were the rulers of ancient Egypt and occupied both political and religious roles. Royal authority was closely connected with the maintenance of order, temple patronage, military leadership, taxation, monumental construction, and the administration of the state.

Egyptian kings ruled across many dynasties over thousands of years, so the title does not describe a single type of reign. Some rulers, such as Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Ramesses II, and Tutankhamun, became especially famous for unusual political, religious, military, or archaeological reasons. Pyramids were mainly associated with earlier periods and were not the universal burial form of every pharaoh.
