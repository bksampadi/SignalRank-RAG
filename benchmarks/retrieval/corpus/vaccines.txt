Vaccines prepare the immune system to recognize a pathogen before a future exposure causes disease. They present an antigen, genetic instruction, or another safe representation of the pathogen so that immune cells can develop memory. Later exposure can then trigger a faster and more effective immune response.

Vaccination is preventive rather than an antibiotic treatment for an existing bacterial infection. The two approaches can both reduce disease burden, but they act at different stages: vaccines reduce the chance or severity of future infection, while antibiotics target susceptible bacteria during an infection.
